What you actually do

Five steps, and four of them are you reading

You record the meeting however you already do. Everything after the drop is automatic — the app tells you what stage it's on, and you come back to a finished document.

A 1-hour meeting is typically ready in 10–15 minutes. You can close the tab — it keeps working.
Under the hood

How sound becomes a labelled transcript

Two engines, same result shape. The local engine does everything on your machine. The cloud engine sends audio to Google for the words, but still does timing and speaker detection locally — because that's the only way to make either trustworthy.

Choose per upload

Local or cloud — you decide, meeting by meeting

Switch in Setup. Confidential meeting? Keep it local. Weak laptop or in a hurry? Use the cloud. The rest of the app behaves identically.

💻 Local — Whisper large-v3

Default · free forever
  • Audio never leaves your laptop; works fully offline
  • Exact timestamps, straight from the model
  • Best accuracy for Malay + English code-switching
  • ~4.5× realtime on the RTX 4050 (1 hr ≈ 13 min)
  • Needs an NVIDIA GPU to be fast (CPU works, slower)
  • One-time 3 GB model download

☁️ Cloud — Google Gemini

Opt-in · your own API key · Google may bill you
  • No GPU needed — runs on any machine or Linux server
  • Fast: 15 min meeting in ~2 min
  • Your recording is uploaded to Google (~48 h retention)
  • Timestamps reconstructed locally (~2 s slack, not exact)
  • Speakers still detected on your machine
  • Needs internet + a key from aistudio.google.com
Tech stack

Everything open, nothing locked in

Plain files on disk, no database, no build step, no npm. You can read every artefact the app produces with Notepad.

🗣️ Whisper large-v3

Speech → text

OpenAI's multilingual model — the strongest open option for Malay, English and mixing the two mid-sentence. Run via faster-whisper / CTranslate2 on the GPU.

👥 pyannote 3.0 + TitaNet

Who spoke when

Segmentation splits speech turns; TitaNet-Large fingerprints each voice; clustering groups them. Runs on CPU through sherpa-onnx — no PyTorch, no HF token.

🔈 Silero VAD

Where speech is

Tiny CPU model. Skips silence for the local engine, and provides the timeline that makes cloud transcripts line up with the audio.

🎛️ ffmpeg

Audio plumbing

Reads anything. High-pass kills room rumble; speech normalization lifts people sitting far from the mic.

☁️ Gemini 2.5 Flash

Optional cloud ASR

Strong on Malay. Used for words only, with your own API key, only if you switch it on.

🧠 Ollama · qwen2.5

Summaries

If installed, writes the summary in the meeting's own language. If not, a built-in extractive summary still works.

⚡ FastAPI + uvicorn

Backend

One background worker, one queue. Interrupted jobs re-queue themselves when the server restarts.

📄 Vanilla HTML/CSS/JS

Frontend

Single file, no framework, no build. Opens instantly and will still run in five years.

Straight answers

What it can't do

Worth knowing before you trust a transcript in a meeting minute.

🗣️🗣️ Two people at once

One microphone records one mixed waveform. When voices overlap, the words go to the dominant speaker. No current system fully separates them from a single-mic recording — cloud included.

🔢 Auto speaker count

Echoey rooms make one voice look like several. If you know how many people spoke, type it in — it beats auto-detect every time.

⏱️ Cloud timing

Reconstructed, not measured: ~2 s median error, ~7 s worst. Local timestamps are exact. Use local if you need frame-accurate subtitles.

🔇 Very distant voices

Speech normalization lifts them, but if a human listener can't make out the words, neither can the model.
